(soft, small — a child, not sad, just saying it) I didn't eat lunch today. My mom said I shouldn't.

(a pause. then low, weathered, like a man remembering) A few years ago... on a cold night in Ogden, there was a little Christmas village. Snow coming down soft. The whole square strung with light. And that season, it was hosting the Giving Machines. (a breath) A man walked up to them. He didn't look like he had much. Tired, maybe — the kind of tired that doesn't come from one bad day. But he'd walked a long way to get there. (gentler) The machines glowed red in the snow, and they told him what ten dollars could do. How ten dollars could feed a child for a week. A whole week of lunches... for a kid who'd otherwise go without.

(quieter — the man, almost to himself) Somebody did that for me once.

(narrator, softer still, like letting you in on something) And for just a moment... he wasn't a grown man in the snow. He was a boy again. Eight years old. Alone at the end of a long table. (a breath) And a tray slid in front of him — from a hand he never saw. He never found out who. (slow) That was the whole point.

(back in the cold, gentle) He tried to pay. He held out a folded twenty. There was no change for it. (a small pause) So he bought two. (softer, letting it go) And he walked back into the night... and we never learned his name.

(a turn — warmer light now, plainer, present-tense) This is Mateo. He's eight. He's in a Davis County classroom right now. (a beat) Mateo's family is behind. Not by much. By enough. (slow, careful) And they've decided — the way frightened people decide things — that it's safer for him to be hungry... than to be seen. So he folds his hands. He waits out the lunch hour. (quiet, the worst part) He's good at it.

(gentle) And he's not the only one. When help comes, some of them flinch from it. Because a free tray means a name gets written down. They've learned that being hungry feels safer than being seen.

(even, matter-of-fact, letting the math be mercy) Ten dollars buys a week. Forty, a month. Three hundred and sixty dollars... buys a child a whole year of lunches they would not otherwise eat.

(quiet again, tender) Then somebody slides a tray in front of him. He doesn't see who. He never will. (a beat) That's the point. (softer) No one called his parents. No one said his name. He just... got to eat.

(slow, pulling back, with weight) There are kids in our schools right now who aren't eating. Not because the food isn't there. Because the shame is.

(the loop — slow, three faces) Three children. The same mercy. (the last line lands alone, almost a whisper) The kid you feed... is the one who comes back.
