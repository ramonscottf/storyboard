## PROPS

### Donation Cards
A grid of donation cards behind the glass of a Giving Machine — each a small printed card showing an item (a meal/school lunch, clean water, a coat, school supplies, a chicken). Warmly lit from within the machine. One card subtly highlighted as the school-lunch / "feed a child for a week" option. Clean, hopeful, real — like the actual Light the World cards.

### Twenty-Dollar Bill
A single creased, well-worn U.S. twenty-dollar bill, folded once, held out in a hand. Softened, slightly abstracted (not a forgeable photoreal bill) — it reads clearly as a $20 without being a literal scan. The crease and wear matter: this is money that was held onto.

### Lunch Tray
A school cafeteria lunch tray — a simple molded tray with a basic warm meal (entrée, a side, a roll), a carton of milk. Ordinary, unremarkable, the most normal thing in the world — which is the whole point. Two versions in play: a beige 1980s molded tray (flashback) and a present-day tray (Mateo).

### Backpack
An ordinary kid's school backpack, slightly worn, still on Mateo's shoulders as he sits — he never took it off, because some part of him is always ready to be unnoticed and gone. Plain, real, not branded.
