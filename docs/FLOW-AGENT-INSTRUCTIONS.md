## 1 — World style  *(Reference: a watercolor village plate)*
Every background is loose hand-painted watercolor on textured paper: soft layered washes, feathered edges, colored shadows (blue/violet, never black), tinted whites (never pure white), a cool muted base with warmth only at light sources. Tender storybook-illustration finish — never flat digital color, cel-shading, 3D, or photo-real. Match the attached reference for the painting hand.

## 2 — Characters are a different layer  *(Reference: the Mateo or Boy character sheet)*
Characters are clean, soft 2D cel-animation cartoons that sit ON TOP of the watercolor world — crisper than the painted background, but still soft: rounded forms, thin linework, flat warm color, minimal detail, SMALL SIMPLE eyes (never big anime eyes, never realistic). Characters are NOT watercolor. Match the attached character reference exactly.

## 3 — Recognition engine + wardrobe
Wardrobe is the story's recognition device. The Boy (1980s) and the Man are the SAME person: olive/green top + the SAME blue backpack + brown hair — match them by clothes and pack, NOT by eye color. Mateo is a different child: red/rust hoodie, green backpack, curly hair, dark eyes. Never give the Boy or Man "hazel-eye" matching; never dress Mateo in olive + blue.

## 4 — Era grades (never mix)
Three light grades tell the audience the era: the Ogden night market = cold lavender-blue winter night with warm pooled glow, snow, string lights. The 1980s flashback cafeteria = warm sepia memory tone. The present-day cafeteria = bright, cool-clean daylight.

## 5 — Craft rules
Present-day loneliness is being UNSEEN IN A FULL ROOM — keep cafeterias busy with kids; the lonely child is the only one in focus while the crowd stays soft / semi-silhouette. The 1980s flashback is the opposite: a sparse, dreamy memory where the room falls away. The 1980s and modern cafeterias are the SAME school remodeled — keep shared bones: windows on the left, serving line on the back wall, utensil caddy in the front-left corner. The hand that gives food is ALWAYS off-frame (the hand is the audience). No gimmick camera — no curtain reveals, letter-rises, halos, or ken-burns; heroes stay still, the world breathes.

## 6 — Trays + the Giving Machines
Lunch tray color marks the era: TEAL = 1980s, WHITE/cream = present day. The Light the World Giving Machines stay brand-accurate (cherry-red body, glowing grid of donation cards, wordmark, Church attribution) and sit as a ~15% corner anchor, never centered; overlay the real wordmark in post for any readable text.
