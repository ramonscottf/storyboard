# Lunch Angels — Character Bible

*Locked 2026-05-19. **Recast locked 2026-05-21** (Scott). Five characters. Each has a canon description + a model-sheet render prompt.*

## THE RECOGNITION ENGINE — read this first (changed 2026-05-21)

The Man and the Boy are the same person, 35 years apart, and the audience must *recognize* that. **The recognition is carried by WARDROBE + BACKPACK, not eye color.** Both wear the same olive/green top and **the same blue backpack**, same brown hair. A grown man still carrying the backpack he had as a hungry kid is the rhyme — and it reads instantly on screen, where an iris hue never could.

> Why this changed: the old canon hung the match on "hazel eyes." AI image/video models cannot reliably hold an eye color across shots, and the new uploaded sheets (the Man, the Boy) read dark-eyed. The olive-top + blue-pack rhyme is stronger storytelling AND more reliable to produce. **Hazel-eye matching is RETIRED.** If you see it referenced in a SUPERSEDED doc, ignore it.

Mateo is deliberately distinct — **red/rust hoodie, green backpack, curly hair, dark eyes** — so he reads as his *own* kid. He is the *next* one, not part of the Man↔Boy thread.

Render rule: clean 2D CEL characters on top of loose WATERCOLOR worlds (see FLOW-STYLE-LOCK-v4.md). Characters are NOT watercolor.

## The Lunch Angel — The Stranger (= The Man)

ROLE: the heart of Act 1. He approaches the Giving Machines on a winter night, learns ten dollars feeds a kid, walks away, comes back with a folded $20 and the line that titles the film: 'Let's get two, then. Somebody did this for me.' He is the grown version of The Boy — **same olive top, same blue backpack, same brown hair.** DIGNITY NOTE (the ethical foundation of the whole film): he must NEVER look pitiful, dirty, or theatrical. He looks like someone's brother who had a hard year. Render him with the SAME warmth and care as the volunteer — kinship, not pity.

AGE/BUILD: 30s–40s, lean, gently built, slightly hunched against the cold. FACE: kind tired face, brown hair, **light stubble (NOT a full beard — the beard pulled the model gritty; keep it a soft shape)**, small simple warm eyes. WARDROBE (canon base = his model sheet): olive-green hoodie, **blue backpack on both shoulders**, brown trousers, plain sneakers. For the cold night-market shots an open dark coat MAY layer over the hoodie — **but the blue backpack stays on and readable**, because it's the recognition link. PALETTE: olive + muted blues; warm only where the machine-glow touches him. POSTURE: hands in pockets, shoulders forward against the cold; moves carefully, deliberately — a man used to being watched and judged.

## The Boy — Flashback (1980s) (= The Man, age 8)

ROLE: the keystone. He is the Man, 35 years earlier — the kid a stranger once fed. Shown in the #7 flashback and again in the #23 loop. **He must read as the SAME person as the Man: same olive top, same blue backpack, same brown hair.** That recognition is the engine of the film.

AGE/BUILD: 8–9, small, slightly built. FACE: brown hair, small simple warm dark eyes, soft cheek blush, a mouth just beginning to open into relief. WARDROBE: olive/green crewneck sweater + **blue backpack** (matching the Man's). PALETTE: the 1980s/memory feel comes from the SCENE — warm sepia grade on the old-cafeteria plate, softer/yellower than present day — NOT baked into the character sheet. POSTURE: alone at a cafeteria table, hands in lap, looking up as a tray (teal — see era note) slides in from off-frame.

## Doug — The Volunteer

ROLE: the Giving Machine host — accurate to the real program, where volunteers welcome visitors at the kiosks. He witnesses the moment that becomes the legend. Not a saint, not a salesman — a regular kind man on a cold night, who will tell this story for the rest of his life. EMOTIONAL INTENT: warmth without pity; treats the stranger as an equal.

AGE/BUILD: late 60s, average build, comfortable in himself. FACE: white short-cropped beard, kind eyes, easy unforced smile. HAIR: short grey. WARDROBE: base = tan cardigan over a light-blue collared shirt, dark trousers, brown loafers. **Outdoor/cold variant = navy fur-collar parka over the same** (use this for the night-market scene). PALETTE: warm tans, oatmeal, soft grey. POSTURE: relaxed, hands in pockets or gesturing plainly; leans in to listen, never looms.

## The Giving Machines — Light the World

ROLE: the third, silent character of Act 1 — MORE than background. The warm, glowing source of the mercy the film is about; the still point the Man walks toward. Treat them with reverence, not as set dressing. Their GLOW is their expression — the 'Light of the World' motif made literal.

WHAT THEY ARE (accuracy is non-negotiable — this film hopes the Church may share it): the real Light the World Giving Machines of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. A row of THREE cherry-red kiosks shaped like vending machines, but instead of snacks the illuminated faces show grids of donation CARDS (food, clean water, clothing, education, livestock). Official 'Giving Machines' wordmark; cherry/cardinal red bodies; warm internal light spilling out. The Church covers all costs so 100% of every donation goes to charity (context for tone, not shown). In our story, one card is the school-lunch donation — the seed of Lunch Angels.

PALETTE: cherry red + warm gold glow against lavender-blue snowy night. PRESENCE: they sit in fresh snow, light pooling on wet pavement; crisper and more luminous than the loose painted village behind them — a character, not scenery.

## Mateo

ROLE: the child at the heart of the present-day story. Eight, a Davis County student, whose family is behind and afraid of attention, so he doesn't eat. He is NOT the Man and NOT the Boy — he is the NEXT one, the kid we feed today who could be the somebody who comes back. EMOTIONAL INTENT: not crying, not pitiful — QUIET. Patient. Careful. The kind of patient that doesn't belong on an eight-year-old; an eight-year-old practicing invisibility.

AGE/BUILD: 8, real-proportioned. FACE: **curly brown hair, DARK eyes** (his own — distinct from the Man↔Boy thread on purpose), soft cheek blush, a small calm mouth-line. WARDROBE: **a slightly oversized red/rust hoodie, green backpack on both shoulders**, ordinary trousers, plain sneakers. PALETTE: brighter, more saturated present-day daylight (NOT memory tone). POSTURE: backpack still on his shoulders, hands folded in his lap, looking down at the empty table where a tray would go.
